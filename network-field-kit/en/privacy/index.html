Network Field Kit Privacy Policy

Effective: November 7, 2026 / Last updated: November 7, 2026 / Provider: Mayudama Factory / 日本語

Mayudama Factory (“we”, “us”) provides “Network Field Kit” (the “App”), a network diagnostics app for iOS and iPadOS. This policy explains how information is handled in the App. This page is a translation of the Japanese policy; if the two differ, the Japanese version applies.

1. Information we receive

The App has no server operated by us. We do not receive your information and we have no access to it. Diagnostic results, history, bookmarks, test plans and settings are stored on your device. Only if you turn on sync with Pro, history, bookmarks, report drafts, test plans and names you give LAN devices are also stored in your own iCloud (settings are not synced). Network traffic goes directly from your device to the destination you specify or to the third-party services listed in Section 2.

3. Information handled on your device

  • History of diagnostic results, bookmarks, settings such as thresholds, pinned tools, names of devices you register, names you give LAN devices (a MAC or IP address and a name; Pro), and the destinations of remote Wake-on-LAN (host name, port, MAC address) are stored on your device (in the App’s storage).
  • Test plans (name, notes, steps, pass/fail criteria, and target host names or IP addresses; SNMP community strings are not saved) and their results (one history entry per run, with the pass/fail of each step, measured values and each tool’s result). If a plan includes a Network info step, the SSID and public IP address are included in history as that step’s result. If it includes LAN discovery or port scan steps, the devices and ports found are included too. A test plan PDF shows only a match or mismatch for the criteria on the SSID and the public IP address range, and does not print the SSID or public IP address. A PDF you export, share or print goes to the place you choose (the App does not send it).
  • Results of the routing table, ARP and VPN/proxy screens. They are saved only when you tap “Save to history” (ARP results include MAC addresses and device names; they do not include public IP addresses).
  • The name of the Wi-Fi network you are connected to (SSID). It is read to show on the Home screen and may be saved with a diagnostic result in history. (The walk test records only that the connection changed, not the SSID.) On iOS, reading the SSID requires the Location permission (While Using). The App does not use your location, and does not record or send it.
  • SNMP community strings. What you enter is kept only in the App’s memory. It is not saved, and not sent anywhere except in the SNMP request to the destination you entered. It does not appear in history, sharing, CSV/JSON export or bookmarks, and disappears when the App quits.
  • Widgets. Widgets do not communicate. They only show the public IP address and connection state the App has already obtained, shared on your device. If the IP address is shown on a Lock Screen or Home Screen, other people looking at your device may see it. The display is hidden while the device is locked. Widgets are a Pro feature.
  • Shortcuts. You can run diagnostics such as Ping and DNS from Shortcuts. The result is handed only to Shortcuts on your device and does not reach us. The network traffic of the diagnostic you run is as described in Section 2. So that you can pick a plan in “Run test plan”, the App keeps a copy of only the plan’s ID and name on your device (not its steps or criteria).
  • Local Network permission. Only when you use features such as LAN discovery, iOS asks for permission after the App explains why. It is used to reach devices on the same LAN (including name lookups; see Section 2) and to read the device’s ARP table. MAC addresses and device names read from the ARP table are not sent off the device.
  • MAC addresses. iOS does not give apps the MAC address of your own device or information about nearby access points, so the App does not obtain them. For other devices on your LAN, the App shows what iOS provides. The MAC addresses of other devices are read on your device only to show the LAN device list and are not sent off the device (with remote Wake-on-LAN, the MAC address you enter as the target is sent to the destination you entered).
  • Purchases. Purchases of Pro are processed by Apple. The App does not handle payment card information.

4. Sync with iCloud (Pro, optional)

Only if you turn on sync with Pro, history (which may include SSIDs), bookmarks, report drafts, test plans and names you give LAN devices are saved to your own iCloud private database and synced with other devices signed in with the same Apple ID. Settings such as thresholds and pinned tools are not synced and stay on your device only. Sync is turned on only after you confirm in a dialog in the App, and is off by default. We do not receive this data and cannot read it. How iCloud handles data is governed by Apple’s terms and privacy policy.

5. Advertising and consent

The free version shows one ad from Google AdMob (Google LLC) at the bottom of the screen. Ads are not shown if you buy Pro. On a launch where the App confirms you have bought Pro, it does not initialize the advertising SDK and does not make any ad-related connections.

  • To deliver ads, measure results and prevent fraud, the advertising SDK sends the advertising identifier (IDFA), device information (model, OS version, language, etc.), IP address (which may be used to estimate approximate location), and how ads are viewed and used, to Google. This information does not reach us.
  • The App may ask for permission to track you using Apple’s App Tracking Transparency. Only if you allow it does the advertising SDK use the IDFA to personalize ads and measure results. If you do not allow it, ads are still shown, but the IDFA is not used and ads are not personalized. You can change this at any time in Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking.
  • Ads are personalized only for users whose App Store country or region is Japan, the United States, the EEA, the United Kingdom or Switzerland. For users in other countries and regions, the App does not ask for tracking permission and always shows non-personalized ads (the IDFA is not used).
  • For users in the European Economic Area (EEA), the United Kingdom and Switzerland, a consent prompt from Google’s consent management platform (UMP) is shown before ads are loaded. For users in the United States, a message about choices under state privacy laws is shown. You can review your choice in the App’s Settings under “Privacy settings (ads)”, an item shown depending on your region.
  • How Google handles information is governed by Google’s Privacy Policy.
  • All features of the App work even if you do not allow tracking.

6. Children’s privacy

The App is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child has provided information, please contact us at the address in Section 12.

7. Retention and deletion

  • History, bookmarks, test plans, names you give LAN devices and settings: kept on your device until you delete them. You can delete history and bookmarks within the App. Deleting the App also deletes the data on your device.
  • iCloud sync data: deleting history and other items in the App while sync is on is reflected in iCloud. Turning sync off does not delete data that is already in iCloud. To remove data that remains in iCloud, delete it in the App while sync is still on, or delete it from Settings > your name > iCloud > Manage Storage.
  • The cached copy of IANA’s public data: refreshed within 24 hours at most, and removed when you delete the App.
  • The first consent for port scan: stored on your device, and you can withdraw it in the App’s Settings. The confirmation for port scans to non-private addresses and SNMP community strings are held in memory only for that run, and are neither saved nor sent.
  • Speed test consent: you can review and withdraw it in the App’s Settings under “Speed test consent”. If you withdraw, you are asked again before the next speed test.
  • Information that has reached third parties (speed-test traffic that reached Cloudflare, information the advertising SDK sent to Google, etc.): we cannot delete it. Please check each operator’s policy.

8. Disclosure to third parties

We do not provide your information to third parties except as required by law. As described in Sections 2 and 5, however, information is sent from your device to third-party services for the diagnostics you run and for ads.

9. Third-party libraries and services

The App uses the Google Mobile Ads SDK and the Google User Messaging Platform for ads. Third-party data and services used by the App are also listed in the App’s Settings under “Licenses & Sources”.

10. Information we receive when you contact us

If you email us, we receive your email address, your message, and details you tell us such as the device, OS and App version. We use it only to reply and to investigate problems, and we do not provide it to third parties.

11. Changes to this policy

We may change this policy as needed. When we do, we will publish the change on this page and update the date.
